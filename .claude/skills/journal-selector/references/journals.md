# Journal Knowledge (qualitative layer)

This file holds the **qualitative** knowledge web search will not give you quickly: each venue's editorial taste (what angle it rewards — the input to Dimension 1), tier grouping, a publisher / sponsoring-society / country table, submission portals, and the institutional-blacklist caution list.

**All numeric data (IF, H-index, CiteScore, quartile, acceptance rate, review time, OA status) is obtained live by web search** (there is no bundled numeric database). Do not keep or cite fixed numbers here — if a number is needed, web-search the current value from JCR/Scopus so there is a single, current source of truth. The tier groupings below are editorial-reputation buckets for judgment, not a substitute for the current IF/quartile.

Codes in "Wants": **M** = method/technical, **B** = biology/mechanism, **C** = clinical/translational.

**Scope note.** Editorial-taste depth is greatest for proteomics, MS, spatial omics, oncology, and clinical translation (the original cores). Coverage now also spans the major Nature/Science/Cell family sub-journals and field flagships in neuroscience, immunology, microbiology/microbiome, hepatology & gastroenterology, cardiology, urology, hematology, extracellular vesicles, materials/nanomedicine, and the high-impact China-based venues. For fields with only a venue-identification entry here (no deep taste note), still web-search the venue's current scope before leaning on it.

## Tier S — Multidisciplinary flagships

- **Nature / Science / Cell** — paradigm-level B or M; broad significance; desk-rejection risk dominates, so selling-point match must be "broadly significant," not just "very good."

## Tier A — Top specialist / Nature-family & elite society

- **Nature Methods** — M; the method *is* the paper, with demonstrated broad utility.
- **Nature Biotechnology** — M, C; enabling tech with translational/commercial reach.
- **Nature Medicine** — C; clinically impactful, often human cohorts/trials.
- **Nature Biomedical Engineering** — M, C; engineering / device / diagnostic platforms with demonstrated biomedical impact; the engineering advance must carry the paper.
- **Nature Cancer** — B, C; cancer biology with significance.
- **Cancer Cell** — B, C; mechanistic cancer biology, complete story.
- **Cancer Discovery** — B, C; high-impact cancer biology plus translational breakthrough (AACR flagship); wants a conceptual advance with disease relevance.
- **Molecular Cell** — B; mechanistic molecular biology.
- **Nature Cell Biology** — B; cell-biology mechanism, complete and broadly significant story.
- **Nature Genetics** — B; genetics/genomics of disease and traits, large-scale and statistically rigorous.
- **Nature Immunology** — B; immunology mechanism, broad significance.
- **Nature Neuroscience** — B; neuroscience mechanism, molecular to systems.
- **Nature Microbiology** — B; microbiology and microbiome mechanism.
- **Nature Metabolism** — B, C; metabolism physiology and metabolic disease.
- **Nature Aging** — B, C; aging biology and translational aging.
- **Nature Protocols** — M; mature, reproducible, broadly useful protocol.
- **Science Translational Medicine** — C; mechanism-to-clinic translational work with a clear path to patients.
- **Science Immunology** — B; immunology with broad significance (AAAS).
- **Immunity** — B; immunology mechanism.

## Tier B — High-impact generalist & strong specialist

- **Nature Communications** — M, B, C; broad, solid; the workhorse high-impact venue.
- **Communications Biology** — M, B; broad sound-science (Nature Portfolio OA); solid biology/methods without the broad-significance bar of Nature Communications.
- **Science Advances** — M, B, C; broad significance.
- **Advanced Science** — M, B, C; broad multidisciplinary (materials ↔ bio ↔ medicine), Wiley OA; rewards a clear advance with broad reach.
- **National Science Review** — M, B, C; China's flagship broad-science venue (CAS); high-significance work across disciplines.
- **Molecular & Cellular Proteomics (MCP)** — M, B; proteomics field flagship; rewards rigorous methods and complete data deposition, rejects weak data standards regardless of biology.
- **EMBO Molecular Medicine** — C, B; molecular medicine, translational.
- **The EMBO Journal** — B; rigorous molecular-biology mechanism, complete story (EMBO Press).
- **Genome Biology** — M, B; genomics/computational methods + biology.
- **Cell Reports Medicine** — C; translational, clinically oriented; wants patient-level relevance.
- **Cell Systems** — M, B; systems-level, quantitative.
- **Genome Medicine** — C; clinical genomics/multi-omics.
- **Molecular Systems Biology** — M, B; systems biology, networks.
- **Nucleic Acids Research** — M, B; nucleic-acid biology, computational methods, and databases / web servers (the annual Database & Web Server issues).

## Cell-biology & developmental family (broad mechanism)

- **Cell Metabolism** — B; metabolism biology, complete story.
- **Developmental Cell** — B; developmental and cell-biology mechanism, complete story.
- **Cell Stem Cell** — B; stem-cell and regenerative biology mechanism.
- **Current Biology** — B; broad biology across scales, faster turnaround than Cell.
- **Neuron** — B; neuroscience mechanism (Cell Press), molecular to circuit.
- **Cell Host & Microbe** — B; host–pathogen and microbiome mechanism; often expects in vivo evidence (evidence-bar cap applies for in-vitro-only stories).

## Tier C — Solid field journals & methods venues

- **Cell Reports** — B; solid biology, less broad than Cell.
- **Analytical Chemistry** — M; analytical novelty and performance metrics; a biology story with no analytical advance is a poor fit.
- **Journal of Proteome Research (JPR)** — M; methods, data analysis, resource datasets.
- **Clinical Proteomics** — C; clinical/translational proteomics.
- **JACS** — M; strong chemistry novelty (probes, crosslinkers, chemical proteomics), not clinical cohorts or pure data analysis.
- **J Am Soc Mass Spectrom (JASMS)** — M; MS instrumentation and fundamentals.
- **iScience** — M, B, C; broad, faster turnaround.
- **Oncogene** — B, C; molecular cancer biology / mechanism; reasonable mid-tier cancer target.
- **Clinical Cancer Research** — C; clinical oncology, translational.

## Tier D — Broad-scope specialist (moderate selectivity)

- **Proteomics / Journal of Proteomics** — M, B; general proteomics, wide scope.
- **Scientific Reports / PLOS ONE** — any; technically sound, not novelty-gated; broad.

## General & internal medicine (pure clinical research)

The top general-medicine journals, where pure clinical research (trials, large cohorts, practice-changing evidence) lands. They reward clinical significance and rigor (randomized design, large N, hard endpoints), not molecular novelty — a proteomics or mechanism angle is usually secondary or absent here. Selling-point match is high only for genuinely clinical studies with practice implications.

- **New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM)** — C; the highest bar for practice-changing clinical evidence, especially pivotal trials.
- **The Lancet** — C; major clinical trials and global-health evidence with broad significance.
- **JAMA** — C; clinical trials, cohorts, and health-policy research for a broad physician audience.
- **The BMJ** — C; clinical research, methodology, and health-policy work; values generalizability and reporting rigor.
- **Annals of Internal Medicine** — C; internal-medicine clinical research and guidelines.
- **Lancet / JAMA specialty family** (Lancet Oncology, Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, JAMA Oncology, JAMA Internal Medicine, etc.) — C; high-impact clinical research within a specialty; strong targets when the work is clinical but field-specific rather than general-medicine.

## Use with caution — institutional blacklist & quality risk

Several high-volume journals are friendly to pure-bioinformatics or rapid-turnaround papers but appear on the institutional blacklists of many Chinese institutions, or carry quality/reputation risk. **Before submitting, check your own institution's current blacklist and policy** — this list is descriptive, not an endorsement or a definitive predatory label. Acceptance tends to be easier and faster, but a blacklisted publication may not count for evaluation, hiring, or funding.

Commonly flagged or scrutinized: Oncotarget, Oncology Reports, OncoTargets and Therapy, Cancer Management and Research, Medical Science Monitor, Oncology Letters, Molecular Medicine Reports, BioMed Research International, Journal of Cancer, Journal of Cellular Biochemistry, Journal of Cellular Physiology, Bioscience Reports, Gene, Cancer Medicine, Medicine (Baltimore), Pathology Research and Practice, and various MDPI/Frontiers titles (e.g. Cancers (Basel), Frontiers in Oncology/Genetics, IJMS) depending on the institution.

When asked to rank or evaluate one of these: score it normally on the three dimensions, but **add an explicit caution** that it may be blacklisted at the user's institution and they must verify against their own list. Never silently recommend one as a top choice.

The China-based flagships in the section above (STTT, Cell Research, Cell Discovery, National Science Review, Cancer Communications, Bone Research, Cellular & Molecular Immunology) are NOT caution titles — they are well-regarded and do not carry the blacklist concern.

## Reading a third-party aggregator card (hard vs soft data)

Chinese aggregator cards (LetPub, MedSci, etc.) bundle reliable and unreliable fields together. When a user pastes one:
- **Hard / usable:** the multi-year IF history is JCR-sourced — read the trend (rising vs falling), but confirm the current value against JCR.
- **Source-flagged:** a small "H-index" value on these cards is often a short-window figure, not the Scopus/SJR journal H-index (which is in the hundreds for an established journal). Note which source any H-index came from.
- **Soft / directional only:** review time and acceptance rate feed Dimension 3 but should be confirmed via the journal's author guidelines.
- **Prediction, not fact:** any "predicted IF" / "Sanger prediction" value is a third-party forecast.
