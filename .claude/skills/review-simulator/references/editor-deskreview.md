# Editor Desk Review and Synthesis

Read this for the editor role. The editor is a decision-maker and gatekeeper, not a third reviewer. Two jobs: the desk-review triage before external review, and the synthesis of the reviews into a decision.

## Desk review (triage): should this be sent out?

Read the manuscript quickly and judge, before committing reviewers' time:

- **Aims and scope fit.** Does the paper belong in this journal? A clear mismatch is a desk-reject.
- **Novelty and importance against the journal's bar.** Does the advance clear this journal's threshold? For high-impact journals this is the decisive gate. Ask the "so what" for the journal's readers and how strong the priority is.
- **First-pass plausibility.** Do the methods and conclusions hold up on a quick read, or is there an obvious fatal flaw?
- **Compliance and completeness.** Ethics approval and consent where applicable, data and code availability, competing-interests statement, formatting, and, where visible, image integrity. Missing essentials can hold up or sink a submission.

**Decision:** desk-reject or send for review. If it is clearly out of scope or below the bar, desk-reject promptly with clear reasons; a fast honest rejection respects the author's time far more than a two-month wait. Only papers that clear this gate go to reviewers.

In simulation, state the desk call and the specific reasons, and if it would be desk-rejected, say so plainly (the author still benefits from knowing why).

## Editor synthesis (after the reviews)

- Read the manuscript and all reviewer reports, then form your own editorial judgment.
- **Weigh by argument quality, not vote count.** One reviewer who explains their reasoning outweighs two who only say "not novel enough".
- Reconcile conflicting reviews; where they cannot both be satisfied, make a reasoned choice and explain it.
- Separate the must-fix core issues from optional suggestions, and prioritize them for the author.
- Give an overall decision: accept / minor revision / major revision / reject (and resubmit) / reject.

## Editor ethics (modeled in the framing)

Confidentiality, fairness, timeliness, and constructive tone; follow recognized misconduct norms (e.g. COPE) if integrity issues surface; an editor with a conflict of interest on a paper should recuse. The simulation reflects this stance rather than acting on real cases.
