# Cover Letter Skill (期刊投稿 Cover Letter 写作)

Based on Wiley editor training: a cover letter is a sales letter to the editor — prove three things with maximum concision: the problem matters, the results are a breakthrough, the paper matches the journal and its readers.

Quality bar: *"A letter isn't good when you can't add more; a letter is good when you can't take away anything more."* (删无可删，而非加无可加。)

## The 6 questions every cover letter must answer

1. Why is this topic important?
2. Why are these results significant?
3. What is the key result? (breakthrough!)
4. Why is it an advance on previous work?
5. Why are you submitting to this journal?
6. Why will this journal's readers read it?

## Standard structure (8 blocks, in order)

1. **Salutation + submission statement** — "Dear Editors," or a named editor (get the name right). "We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled '***' for your consideration as a research article in **Journal name**."
2. **Background + team credibility** — one short paragraph on field progress; establish the authors' track record with "we have cumulatively contributed to … (refs ***)".
3. **Gap paragraph** — use a **"However, …"** turn to state what previous methods fail to do (e.g., only effective in specific device architectures; lacks systematic investigation of heat flow across the whole device).
4. **Core work paragraph** — open with **"Herein, we propose …"**; state the strategy and one or two quantified key results (e.g., "reduces the cell temperature under AM 1.5G by ~6.5 °C, universally applicable to both n–i–p and p–i–n architectures"). Add a **"To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the most superior … ever reported (Table C1)"** claim backed by a comparison table when justified.
5. **Significant findings list** — numbered 1) 2) 3), leading with first-of-kind claims ("For the first time, we propose …").
6. **Strongest data block** — concrete retention/lifetime figures (e.g., "retains 88% and 93% of its initial efficiency after 1776 h of 85 °C thermal aging and 2451 h of MPP tracking").
7. **Journal-fit paragraph** — "Thus, we believe that the impact of this comprehensive discussion makes it worth of publication in **Journal** and attracts broad interest to the audience." + a reader-benefit sentence: "…will offer the readers chemical and practical insights into ***… thereby attracting a broad readership."
8. **Close** — recommend reviewers ("We would like to recommend the following experts as reviewers: ***"), thank the editor, "With my best regards," + corresponding author signature.

## Hard rules / common failure modes

- **Get the editor's name right.** If addressing a specific editor, verify spelling and title.
- **Get the journal's name right — everywhere.** When reusing a letter for a different journal, check *every instance* of the journal name (submission statement, fit paragraph, headers).
- **Don't waffle — get straight to the point.** Cut throat-clearing sentences; every sentence must answer one of the 6 questions.
- Keep the whole letter to roughly one page.

## Workflow

1. Collect from the user: manuscript title, target journal, editor name (if known), key breakthrough + quantified results, advance over prior work, why this journal/readers, suggested reviewers.
2. Draft following the 8-block structure; use the proven phrasings above as anchors but adapt to the paper's actual science.
3. Self-check against the 6 questions and the hard rules; verify every journal/editor name instance; trim to one page.
4. Deliver as editable text (and .docx via the docx skill if the user wants a file).
