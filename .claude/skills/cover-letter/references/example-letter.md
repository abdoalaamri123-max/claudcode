Organic-inorganic hybrid perovskite solar cells have progressed rapidly in recent years, wherein considerable efforts focus on the thin film growth to achieve higher power conversion efficiency and stability in devices. During the past years, we have cumulatively contributed to understand the perovskite absorber and crystal growth to achieve highly efficient and stable materials and devices (****).

The limited lifetimes of perovskite solar cells (PSCs) at elevated temperatures under sunlight illumination have delayed their commercialization. Most reported methods to optimize the thermal and photo-thermal stability of PSCs is to enhance the heat resistance of materials. However, most materials under investigation only take effects in specific device architectures. It lacks systematic investigations to consider the heat flow within the entire device, which is essential for the development of new strategies that can be universally applied to improve the thermal and photo-thermal stability of PSCs.

Herein, we propose a holistic solution to improve heat transfer within the entire PSCs by introducing hexagonal boron nitride (h-BN) inside of absorber and the radiator fin outside of device. This strategy reduces the cell temperature under illumination (AM 1.5G) by ~6.5 °C, which can be universally applied to PSCs with either n–i–p or p–i–n architecture. Moreover, we find that improving heat transfer enables durable PSCs with prolonged lifetime during 85 °C thermal aging or maximum power point (MPP) tracking. To the best of our knowledge, this is one of the most superior stability of PSCs ever reported up to now (Table C1).

The optimized h-BN/Cu device with n–i–p structure retains 88% and 93% of its initial efficiency after 1776 hours of 85 °C thermal aging and 2451 hours of MPP tracking, respectively. These results suggest that improving heat transfer is a prominent and universal way to fabricate highly efficient and durable PSCs, which would further drive their commercialization in the future.
