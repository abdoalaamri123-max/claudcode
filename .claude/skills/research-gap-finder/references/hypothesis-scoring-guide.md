# Hypothesis Quality Assessment Guide

## Scoring Framework

### Innovation Score (1-5)

**Score 1 - Incremental Improvement**
- Direct extension of existing work
- Minor modification to established methods
- Predictable next step in research program
- *Example*: Testing same intervention in slightly different population

**Score 2 - New Application**
- Applying established methods to new context
- Novel combination of existing approaches
- Testing existing theory in different domain
- *Example*: Adapting biomedical technique to environmental science

**Score 3 - Novel Integration**
- Creative combination of distinct approaches
- New perspective on existing problem
- Cross-methodological innovation
- *Example*: Integrating qualitative and quantitative methods in novel way

**Score 4 - Significant Advance**
- New methodological approach
- Theoretical framework innovation
- Paradigm-challenging perspective
- *Example*: Developing entirely new measurement technique

**Score 5 - Paradigm-Shifting**
- Fundamental rethinking of the field
- Revolutionary new approach
- Creates entirely new research direction
- *Example*: Proposing alternative theoretical framework that challenges foundational assumptions

---

### Feasibility Score (1-5)

**Score 1 - Highly Challenging**
- Requires multiple breakthrough technologies
- Extreme resource requirements
- Major technical or ethical barriers
- *Example*: Research requiring prohibited materials or impossible measurements

**Score 2 - Difficult but Possible**
- Significant technical challenges
- Substantial resources needed
- Multiple regulatory hurdles
- *Example*: Large-scale multi-center clinical trial with novel technology

**Score 3 - Moderately Feasible**
- Available methods with modifications
- Reasonable resource requirements
- Some technical challenges
- *Example*: Well-designed study using established methods in novel context

**Score 4 - Highly Feasible**
- Standard methodologies
- Available resources and expertise
- Minimal regulatory barriers
- *Example**: Straightforward experimental study with available equipment

**Score 5 - Immediately Actionable**
- Off-the-shelf methods and resources
- Low cost and time investment
- No major barriers
- *Example*: Data analysis study using existing datasets

---

### Impact Score (1-5)

**Score 1 - Limited Impact**
- Small contribution to narrow subfield
- Minimal practical applications
- Unlikely to influence future research
- *Example*: Minor methodological refinement with limited applicability

**Score 2 - Modest Impact**
- Contributes to specific research area
- Some practical applications
- May influence related work
- *Example*: Study improving existing method for specific use case

**Score 3 - Significant Impact**
- Meaningful contribution to field
- Clear practical applications
- Likely to influence future research directions
- *Example*: Research addressing important gap with wide applicability

**Score 4 - High Impact**
- Major contribution to field
- Substantial practical applications
- Will influence multiple research areas
- *Example*: Foundational research opening new subfield

**Score 5 - Transformative Impact**
- Paradigm-shifting contribution
- Revolutionary practical applications
- Will redefine field directions
- *Example*: Discovery that completely changes understanding of fundamental phenomenon

---

## Priority Determination

### HIGH Priority Hypotheses
- **Criteria**: Innovation ≥4 AND Feasibility ≥3 AND Impact ≥4
- **Action**: Immediate experimental design and grant proposal development
- **Example**: Novel method (4), highly feasible (4), high impact (5)

### MEDIUM Priority Hypotheses
- **Criteria**: Innovation ≥3 OR (Feasibility ≥4 AND Impact ≥3)
- **Action**: Consider after high-priority projects, exploratory work
- **Example**: Moderate innovation (3), very feasible (5), good impact (3)

### LOW Priority (Exploratory)
- **Criteria**: Innovation ≤2 OR Feasibility ≤2
- **Action**: Long-term exploratory consideration, collaboration opportunities
- **Example**: High innovation but very difficult (5, 2, 4)

## Quality Control Checks

### Mandatory Requirements
- [ ] Hypothesis is clearly stated and testable
- [ ] Variables are operationally defined
- [ ] Rationale connects to identified research gap
- [ ] Methodology is scientifically sound
- [ ] Ethical considerations addressed

### Scoring Consistency
- [ ] Scores are justified with specific reasoning
- [ ] Innovation score reflects true novelty, not just complexity
- [ ] Feasibility considers realistic resource constraints
- [ ] Impact assessment balanced between scientific and practical value

### Risk Assessment
- [ ] Technical risks identified and addressed
- [ ] Resource requirements realistically estimated
- [ ] Timeline estimates account for potential setbacks
- [ ] Ethical and regulatory considerations evaluated

**Use this guide consistently to ensure hypothesis quality assessment remains standardized across different research domains and analysis sessions.**
