# Output templates

Use these formats for the deliverables. Adapt language to the user (Chinese/English), but keep the 14 CRediT role labels in English exactly as journals require.

## 1. Eligibility table (lead with this)

One row per person. Columns: Name · Proposed position · CRediT roles (with lead/equal/supporting) · ICMJE criteria (1/2/3/4) · Status · Note.

Example:

| # | Name | Position | CRediT roles | ICMJE 1·2·3·4 | Status | Note |
|---|------|----------|--------------|---------------|--------|------|
| 1 | A. Lee | First | Conceptualization (equal), Investigation (lead), Formal analysis (lead), Visualization (lead), Writing – original draft (lead) | ✓·✓·?·✓ | Incomplete | Must be given the chance to approve final version (crit. 3) |
| 2 | B. Ng | Second | Investigation (supporting), Validation (lead) | ✓·✓·✓·✓ | Author | — |
| 3 | C. Roy | Last / corresponding* | Conceptualization (lead), Supervision, Funding acquisition, Writing – review & editing | ✓·✓·✓·✓ | Author | Qualifies via concept + critical revision, not supervision alone |
| 4 | D. Fox | — | Resources (provided patient samples) | ✗·—·—·— | Acknowledge | Sample provision alone; fails crit. 1 → Acknowledgments |

Use ✓ / ✗ / ? for met / not met / unknown. Mark corresponding with `*`.

## 2. Recommended author order

State it as a line, then justify each slot:

> **Recommended byline:** A. Lee¹, B. Ng, E. Park, C. Roy* *(¹ and B. Ng contributed equally, if intended; * corresponding)*

Then one line per position: who and why. End with any contested calls surfaced for the authors to decide, and the assumptions you made (field convention, who is corresponding).

## 3. CRediT author statement (journal-ready)

Prose or list form, exact labels, one entry per author. Standard journal format:

> **CRediT authorship contribution statement**
> **A. Lee:** Conceptualization, Investigation, Formal analysis, Visualization, Writing – original draft. **B. Ng:** Investigation, Validation, Writing – review & editing. **C. Roy:** Conceptualization, Supervision, Funding acquisition, Writing – review & editing.

Do not include acknowledged (non-author) contributors here.

## 4. Author Contributions paragraph (prose)

The same content journals often print as a paragraph. Keep it faithful to the mapping:

> **Author contributions.** A.L. and B.N. conceived the study. A.L. performed the experiments and analysed the data, with B.N. contributing to validation. A.L. prepared the figures and wrote the original draft. C.R. supervised the project, acquired funding, and critically revised the manuscript. All authors reviewed and approved the final manuscript.

Use initials as journals do. The closing "all authors reviewed and approved" line corresponds to ICMJE criteria 2–3 and should only be written once the authors actually have.

## 5. Acknowledgments wording

For everyone routed out of the author list, one or two sentences so the contribution is not lost:

> **Acknowledgments.** We thank D. Fox for providing the patient samples, and the [Facility] for instrument access. This work was supported by [funder, grant no.]. We thank [name] for language editing.

Group by type (materials/samples, facilities, funding, editing). Keep funding statements separate if the journal has a dedicated Funding section.

## Flags block (always include if any exist)

Collect every `[[NEEDS INPUT: ...]]` and every contested call into a short list at the end, so the user sees at a glance what they must still decide or supply before the byline is final.
