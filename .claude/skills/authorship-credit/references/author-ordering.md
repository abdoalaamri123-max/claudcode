# Author ordering: conventions, equal contribution, and contested calls

Order encodes credit, and unlike ICMJE eligibility it is **not** rule-determined — it is convention plus negotiation. Recommend, justify, and flag; do not manufacture certainty.

## The default convention (life sciences / biomedicine)

Most life-sciences and biomedical journals read the byline like this:

- **First author** — did the most hands-on work; usually led the experiments, analysis, and original draft. The lead contributor.
- **Middle authors** — ordered by descending contribution. Earlier = more.
- **Last author** — the senior author / PI who provided intellectual leadership, funding, and oversight (the "anchor" position). Carries seniority weight comparable to first.
- **Corresponding author** — often the PI (last) or the first author, but it is a separate administrative designation and can be anyone; there can be several.

State that you are applying this convention, since other fields differ.

## Other conventions (apply if the field/journal calls for it)

- **Alphabetical order** — standard in mathematics, economics, particle physics, and some theory fields; contribution is treated as equal by convention. If the user is in such a field, offer this instead of the contribution-weighted order.
- **Contribution-weighted, no special last slot** — some fields do not give the last position senior weight; there the order is simply descending contribution end to end.
- **First-and-last as the two "prime" slots** — the biomedical default above.

If unsure, use the biomedical default and say so, and ask the field if it matters to the user.

## Equal contribution (co-first, co-senior)

When two or more people contributed comparably to the lead role, mark them **equal contribution** (a footnote: "These authors contributed equally"). This is common and legitimate. Points to handle:

- The order *within* an equal-contribution group still appears on the page and still matters for how it is read and cited, so it is not neutral even when marked equal. Flag this to the authors; do not pick the internal order silently when it is genuinely contested.
- Co-senior / co-last (multiple PIs) is also allowed, marked the same way.
- Recommend equal contribution when the CRediT mapping shows two people both at **lead** on the core roles (e.g. both led Investigation and Formal analysis). Do not invent it to smooth over a dispute.

## Corresponding author, again (ordering angle)

Mark the corresponding author(s) explicitly in the recommended byline (conventionally an asterisk). If the user has not said who it is, propose the most likely candidate (usually the PI/senior author) and flag it as the authors' choice. Remember only their affiliation drives publishing-agreement/open-access eligibility.

## How to build the recommendation

1. Rank authors by the weight of their qualifying contribution, using the CRediT mapping (lead > equal > supporting across the core roles: Conceptualization, Investigation, Formal analysis, Methodology, Writing – original draft).
2. Place the clearest lead contributor first; place the senior/PI last (biomedical default).
3. Order the middle by descending contribution.
4. Identify equal-contribution groups where two+ people tie at lead.
5. Mark corresponding author(s).
6. Attach a one-line rationale to each position.

## Flag, do not decide, when

- Two people have near-identical lead contributions and no equal-contribution marking is intended → flag as a first-author contest for the authors to resolve.
- The user's desired order conflicts with the contribution evidence (e.g. wants person X first, but Y clearly did the lead work) → surface the mismatch, explain the convention, and let them decide. Do not just comply, and do not just refuse; give them the reasoning.
- Seniority or politics appears to be driving order against contribution → note it neutrally; the decision is theirs, but they should make it with eyes open.

## What ordering is not

Order does not change ICMJE eligibility. Someone who does not qualify as an author cannot be "put last" to include them; they go to Acknowledgments. Resolve eligibility first, order second.
