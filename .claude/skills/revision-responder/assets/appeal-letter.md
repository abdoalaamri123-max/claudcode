# Appeal (Rebuttal) Letter Template

A formal letter to the handling editor contesting a decision, used only when the authors have genuine, evidence-based grounds (see `references/appeal-mode.md`), not for mere disagreement with the outcome.

Replace every bracketed field. Do not leave a bracket unfilled in a delivered draft; if a value is unknown, carry it as `[[NEEDS AUTHOR INPUT: ...]]`.

---

[Date]

Dear Dr. [Editor last name] (or "Dear Editor" if no handling editor is named),

Re: Manuscript [ID], "[Title]", submitted to [Journal]

[**Respectful opening.** Thank the editor and reviewers for their time, acknowledge the decision without disputing it here, and state the request plainly, e.g. "We thank you and the reviewers for the time given to our manuscript. Having reviewed the decision and the reports carefully, we are writing to respectfully request reconsideration of this decision."]

[**Grounds for appeal, stated up front, in a sentence or two.** State the specific factual error, missed content, procedural issue, or reviewer error the appeal rests on, e.g. "We believe the decision rests in part on a misreading of [specific point], which is already addressed in the manuscript at [location], and we would like the opportunity to clarify this."]

**Point-by-point response to the decisive comments**

[Comment or decision point 1, quoted or closely paraphrased from the decision letter or review.]

Response: [Evidence-based response, citing the manuscript location, data, or literature. Concede the point plainly if it is valid rather than contesting it.]

[Comment or decision point 2.]

Response: [Evidence-based response.]

[Add further points only for the comments that actually drove the decision; do not respond point by point to every minor comment.]

[**What we would revise if reconsidered**, if applicable, e.g. "Should the manuscript be reconsidered, we would be glad to [specific revision] to address [specific concern] more fully."]

[**Respectful close.** Request reconsideration or a fresh assessment, and defer to the editor's judgment, e.g. "We respectfully ask that you consider this appeal and would welcome a fresh assessment of the manuscript, whether by the original reviewers or others at your discretion. We fully respect your final decision either way."]

Sincerely,

[Corresponding author name], on behalf of all authors
[Affiliation]
[Email]

---

*Notes on use (delete before sending):*
- *Appeal only with genuine, evidence-based grounds (a factual error, missed content, procedural unfairness, or a demonstrable reviewer error), never for mere disagreement with the outcome or a wish for another chance.*
- *Stay formal, respectful, and evidence-first throughout; never attack a reviewer's competence or motives in this letter.*
- *Keep it concise and focus only on the decisive points; a long letter reads as grasping.*
- *Route any concern about a reviewer's conflict of interest or misconduct to the editor privately in a separate, factual note, not as an accusation inside this letter.*
- *Most journals grant one appeal per decision; treat this draft as the version to send, not a first pass.*
