# Cover Letter Template (front of package)

This letter sits at the **front** of the response package, before the point-by-point. It has three short movements: (1) a genuine thank you grounded in the actual content of the comments, (2) one or two sentences summarizing the main revisions, and (3) a standard transition telling the reader how the detailed response is organized. Keep the whole thing to roughly half a page.

Replace every bracketed field. Do not leave a bracket unfilled in a delivered draft; if a value is unknown, carry it as `[[NEEDS AUTHOR INPUT: ...]]`.

---

[Date]

Dear Dr. [Editor last name],

Re: Manuscript [ID], "[Title]"

[**Thank you, grounded in the actual comments.** Do not use a generic thank you. Refer to what the reviewers and editor specifically noted or valued, drawn from their real remarks, e.g. "We thank the editor and both reviewers for their careful and constructive assessment, and for recognizing [the specific strength the reviewers named]. We are grateful for the thoughtful comments on [the specific areas raised], which have helped us improve the manuscript."]

[**One or two sentences summarizing the main revisions.** State what you focused on when revising, and be sure to cover any point the editor specifically flagged in the decision letter, e.g. "In response, we have [main change 1] and [main change 2], which together [effect on the manuscript]."]

Below we respond to each comment individually, describe the change made, and, where relevant, reproduce the new or revised manuscript text in shaded boxes for ease of reference. All changes are marked in the manuscript using tracked changes. Newly cited references have been verified and added to the reference list.

We hope the revised manuscript is now suitable for publication in [Journal] and look forward to your decision.

Sincerely,

[Corresponding author name], on behalf of all authors
[Affiliation]
[Email]

---

*Notes on use (delete before submitting):*
- *The thank you must reflect the reviewers' actual words, not a template phrase.*
- *Keep the revision summary to one or two sentences; the detail belongs in the point-by-point.*
- *If the target journal uses pointer style rather than shaded quote boxes, adjust the transition sentence to say you indicate where each change appears in the revised manuscript.*
