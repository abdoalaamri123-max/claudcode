# Quality Control and Skeptical-Reviewer Test

## Common mistakes: do not

- Do not respond only with gratitude.
- Do not claim a change without describing it.
- Do not report empirical results without explaining theoretical relevance.
- Do not provide theoretical discussion without changing the manuscript.
- Do not rely on significance levels alone or ignore effect sizes.
- Do not hide null or unfavorable results.
- Do not dismiss an alternative explanation without testing or discussing it.
- Do not treat every request as equally important.
- Do not respond defensively or accuse a reviewer of misunderstanding.
- Do not depart from the reviewer's terminology without a reason.
- Do not introduce a new construct merely to answer a comment.
- Do not add controls mechanically without explaining their relevance.
- Do not run many loosely related analyses hoping that one produces a favorable result.
- Do not let the response and manuscript contradict one another.
- Do not leave page, table, figure, or statistical placeholders in a submitted response.
- Do not promise future work the current revision cannot deliver.
- Do not claim that a limitation is a strength.
- Do not end a difficult response with vague language.

## Final quality review

Before submission, verify:

- every editor comment receives a response;
- every reviewer comment receives a response;
- every subquestion receives a response;
- every promised manuscript change exists;
- every page reference is correct;
- every table and figure reference is correct;
- every statistic matches analysis output;
- every sample size is consistent;
- every construct uses consistent terminology;
- every response begins with a direct answer;
- every major conceptual concern receives a conceptual response;
- every new analysis has a theoretical or diagnostic rationale;
- every null result is reported accurately;
- every limitation is described honestly;
- every disagreement remains respectful and clear;
- every response explains or makes evident how the paper improved;
- the opening accurately summarizes the revision;
- the response contains no emotional, dismissive, or defensive language;
- manuscript claims do not exceed the evidence;
- response and manuscript tell the same theoretical story;
- no plausible but unverified definition, rationale, intention, or interpretation became submission prose;
- inferred concerns remain planning hypotheses unless supported by comments, artifacts, or author confirmation.

## Skeptical-reviewer test

Read the complete response package from a skeptical reviewer's perspective. Ask:

- Did the authors understand my concern?
- Did they answer my actual question?
- Did they address the theoretical issue rather than merely add an analysis?
- Can I verify where the manuscript changed?
- Are results reported transparently?
- Did the authors narrow claims when necessary?
- Did they explain disagreement respectfully?
- Has the manuscript materially improved?
- Can I recommend acceptance without reconstructing the authors' reasoning?

Revise any response that forces the reviewer to infer what the authors did or why it matters.

## Decision-facilitation check

Confirm that the complete response makes major improvements, evidence, limitations, and reasoning easy for the editor to evaluate. Persuasion should come from reduced ambiguity, not volume.
