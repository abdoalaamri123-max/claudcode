# PRISMA 2020 Checklist — Field Inventory

Source: PRISMA 2020 Statement (Page et al.; *BMJ* / *PLOS Medicine* 2021), the 27-item checklist
for reporting systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Applies regardless of publisher, typically
required **in addition to**, not instead of, any publisher-family form (NP, MCP, CP) that also
applies.

**Scope check first:** applies to systematic reviews (with or without meta-analysis). A narrative/
literature review with no pre-specified, reproducible search-and-selection method does not trigger
this track; a scoping review uses the related PRISMA-ScR extension (not covered here) instead.

**Version note:** PRISMA 2020 is current as of this assessment; no newer edition to check for.
Related extensions exist for protocols (PRISMA-P) and scoping reviews (PRISMA-ScR); note if one of
those applies instead, since their item sets differ from the main checklist below.

Status codes: `✅ Covered` / `⚠️ Partial` / `❌ Missing` / `➖ N/A` / `👤 Author-only`.

## Title
- **1.** Identify the report as a systematic review.

## Abstract
- **2.** See the separate Abstract checklist below (PRISMA has its own 12-item structured-abstract
  checklist, distinct from the main 27).

## Introduction
- **3 (Rationale).** Describe the rationale for the review in the context of existing knowledge.
- **4 (Objectives).** An explicit statement of the objective(s) or question(s) the review
  addresses.

## Methods
- **5 (Eligibility criteria).** Inclusion and exclusion criteria for the review, and how studies
  were grouped for the syntheses.
- **6 (Information sources).** All databases, registers, websites, organisations, reference lists,
  and other sources searched, and the date each was last searched.
- **7 (Search strategy).** Full search strategies for all databases, registers, and websites,
  including filters and limits used.
- **8 (Selection process).** Methods used to decide whether a study met the inclusion criteria,
  including how many reviewers screened each record/report, whether independently, and if
  applicable, details of automation tools used.
- **9 (Data collection process).** Methods used to collect data from reports, including how many
  reviewers collected data from each report, whether independently, and any processes for
  obtaining/confirming data from study investigators.
- **10a (Data items — outcomes).** All outcomes for which data were sought, listed and defined,
  including whether all results compatible with each outcome domain were sought and, if not,
  which results were selected and why.
- **10b (Data items — other variables).** All other variables for which data were sought (e.g.
  participant/intervention/exposure/comparator characteristics, funding sources), listed and
  defined, and any assumptions about missing or unclear information.
- **11 (Risk of bias).** Methods used to assess risk of bias in the included studies, including
  details of the tool(s) used, how many reviewers assessed each study, whether independently, and
  if applicable, details of automation tools used.
- **12 (Effect measures).** For each outcome, the effect measure(s) used in the synthesis or
  presentation of results.
- **13a-13f (Synthesis methods).** a. Processes for deciding which studies were eligible for each
  synthesis. b. Methods for preparing data for presentation/synthesis (e.g. handling of missing
  summary statistics, data conversions). c. Methods used to tabulate/visually display results per
  study and synthesis. d. Methods used to synthesise results, and if meta-analysis was done, the
  model, method for identifying presence/extent of statistical heterogeneity, and software used. e.
  Methods used to explore possible causes of heterogeneity among study results (e.g. subgroup
  analysis, meta-regression). f. Sensitivity analyses used to assess robustness of synthesised
  results.
- **14 (Reporting bias assessment).** Methods used to assess risk of bias due to missing results
  (reporting bias) in a synthesis.
- **15 (Certainty assessment).** Methods used to assess certainty (or confidence) in the body of
  evidence for an outcome.

## Results
- **16a (Study selection).** Results of the search and selection process, from the number of
  records identified to the number of included studies, ideally with a flow diagram.
- **16b (Study selection).** Studies that might appear to meet the inclusion criteria but were
  excluded, and the reason for exclusion of each.
- **17 (Study characteristics).** Cite each included study and present its characteristics.
- **18 (Risk of bias in studies).** Present assessments of risk of bias for each included study.
- **19 (Results of individual studies).** For all outcomes, summary statistics for each group
  (where appropriate) and effect estimates and precision (e.g. CIs), ideally with a structured
  presentation or plot for each study.
- **20a-20d (Synthesis results).** a. Briefly summarise the characteristics and risk-of-bias of
  studies contributing to each synthesis. b. Present results of all statistical syntheses, with
  precision/certainty (e.g. CI, prediction interval); for meta-analysis, specify the summary
  measure, method, and number of studies/participants. c. Present results of any investigations of
  possible causes of heterogeneity. d. Present results of any sensitivity analyses.
- **21 (Reporting biases).** Present assessments of risk of bias due to missing results
  (reporting bias) for each synthesis.
- **22 (Certainty of evidence).** Present assessments of certainty (confidence) in the body of
  evidence for each outcome.

## Discussion
- **23a-23d.** a. Provide a general interpretation of the results in the context of other evidence.
  b. Discuss limitations of the evidence included in the review. c. Discuss limitations of the
  review processes used. d. Discuss implications for practice, policy, and future research.

## Other Information
- **24a (Registration).** Registration information, including the register name and registration
  number, or state that the review was not registered.
- **24b (Protocol).** Where the review protocol can be accessed, or state that one was not
  prepared.
- **24c (Amendments).** Amendments to information provided at registration/in the protocol, with
  the rationale.
- **25 (Support).** Sources of financial or non-financial support for the review, and the role of
  the funders/sponsors.
- **26 (Competing interests).** Competing interests of the review authors.
- **27 (Availability of data, code, and other materials).** Which of the following are publicly
  available and where: the template data collection forms, extracted data used in analyses,
  analytic code, and any other materials used in the review.

## Abstract checklist (separate 12-item structure, distinct from item 2 above)

- **1.** Identify the report as a systematic review.
- **2 (Objectives).** State the main objective(s) or question(s) the review addresses.
- **3 (Eligibility criteria).** Inclusion and exclusion criteria for the review.
- **4 (Information sources).** Databases/registers searched and the date of the last search.
- **5 (Risk of bias).** Methods used to assess risk of bias.
- **6 (Synthesis of results).** Methods used to present/synthesise results.
- **7 (Included studies).** Total number of included studies and participants, and relevant
  characteristics.
- **8 (Synthesis of results).** Main results for each synthesis, with precision if possible (e.g.
  CI).
- **9 (Limitations of evidence).** Main limitations of the evidence included.
- **10 (Interpretation).** General interpretation and important implications.
- **11 (Funding).** Primary source of funding.
- **12 (Registration).** Register name and registration number.

## Flow diagram (required alongside the checklist, item 16a)

The updated PRISMA 2020 flow diagram distinguishes records identified (by database/register vs.
other methods), records screened, reports sought/retrieved, reports assessed for eligibility, and
studies included, with the count and reason for each exclusion. Producing the actual diagram
graphic is out of this skill's scope (mark `👤 Author-only`), but confirm item 16a's narrative
numbers are internally consistent with what the diagram would show, and flag a missing diagram.

## Evidence to look for

"Systematic review" or "meta-analysis" in the title/abstract; a registration number (e.g. PROSPERO)
or a stated protocol; a search-strategy appendix or supplementary table listing databases and
dates; a PRISMA flow diagram figure; a risk-of-bias assessment tool named (e.g. Cochrane RoB 2,
ROBINS-I, GRADE); a funding/support statement.
