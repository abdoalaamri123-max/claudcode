# ARRIVE 2.0 Checklist — Field Inventory

Source: ARRIVE guidelines 2.0 (Percie du Sert et al.; *PLOS Biology* 2020; NC3Rs), for reporting
*in vivo* animal research. Applies regardless of publisher, typically required **in addition to**,
not instead of, any publisher-family form (NP, MCP, CP) that also applies — Nature Portfolio's own
Editorial Policy Checklist explicitly recommends ARRIVE for animal work (see
`references/np-editorial-policy-fields.md`, "Additional policy considerations").

**Scope check first:** applies to studies using live, non-human animals *in vivo*. Cell lines,
tissue obtained post-mortem with no live-animal procedure, or purely computational/in-silico work
do not trigger this track even if animal-derived materials are used elsewhere in the study.

**Version note:** ARRIVE 2.0 (2020) is current as of this assessment; no newer edition to check
for.

Status codes: `✅ Covered` / `⚠️ Partial` / `❌ Missing` / `➖ N/A` / `👤 Author-only`.

The **Essential 10** (items 1-10) are the basic minimum for any manuscript describing animal
research; treat these as principal for this track. The **Recommended Set** (items 11-21) adds
context and best practice; assess them too, but a gap here is lower severity than an Essential-10
gap.

## Essential 10

- **1 (Study design).** a. The groups being compared, including control groups (state the
  rationale if no control group was used). b. The experimental unit (e.g. a single animal, litter,
  or cage of animals).
- **2 (Sample size).** a. The exact number of experimental units allocated to each group, and the
  total number in each experiment; also the total number of animals used. b. How the sample size
  was decided, including details of any a priori sample size calculation.
- **3 (Inclusion and exclusion criteria).** a. Criteria for including/excluding animals
  (or experimental units) during the experiment, and data points during analysis, and whether set
  a priori (state explicitly if none were set). b. For each experimental group, any animals,
  experimental units, or data points not included in the analysis, and why (state explicitly if
  none). c. For each analysis, the exact value of n in each experimental group.
- **4 (Randomisation).** a. Whether randomisation was used to allocate experimental units to
  control and treatment groups, and if so the method used to generate the sequence. b. The
  strategy used to minimise potential confounders (order of treatments/measurements, animal/cage
  location); state explicitly if confounders were not controlled.
- **5 (Blinding).** Who was aware of the group allocation at each stage of the experiment (during
  allocation, conduct, outcome assessment, and data analysis).
- **6 (Outcome measures).** a. Clear definition of all outcome measures assessed (e.g. cell death,
  molecular markers, behavioural changes). b. For hypothesis-testing studies, the primary outcome
  measure (the one used to determine the sample size).
- **7 (Statistical methods).** a. Statistical methods used for each analysis, including software
  used. b. Methods used to assess whether the data met the statistical approach's assumptions, and
  what was done if assumptions were not met.
- **8 (Experimental animals).** a. Species-appropriate details: species, strain and substrain,
  sex, age or developmental stage, and, if relevant, weight. b. Provenance, health/immune status,
  genetic modification status, genotype, and any previous procedures.
- **9 (Experimental procedures).** For each experimental group, including controls, procedures in
  enough detail to allow replication: a. what was done, how, and what was used; b. when and how
  often; c. where (including any acclimatisation periods); d. why (rationale for procedures).
- **10 (Results).** For each experiment, including independent replications: a. summary/descriptive
  statistics per group, with a measure of variability where applicable (e.g. mean and SD, or median
  and range); b. if applicable, the effect size with a confidence interval.

## Recommended Set

- **11 (Abstract).** An accurate summary of the research objectives, animal species/strain/sex,
  key methods, principal findings, and study conclusions.
- **12 (Background).** a. Sufficient scientific background to understand the rationale and
  context, and the experimental approach. b. How the animal species and model address the
  scientific objectives and, where appropriate, relevance to human biology.
- **13 (Objectives).** The research question, objectives, and, where appropriate, specific
  hypotheses being tested.
- **14 (Ethical statement).** Name of the ethical review committee or equivalent that approved the
  use of animals, and any relevant licence/protocol numbers; if not sought or granted, a
  justification.
- **15 (Housing and husbandry).** Housing and husbandry conditions, including any environmental
  enrichment.
- **16 (Animal care and monitoring).** a. Interventions/steps taken to reduce pain, suffering, and
  distress. b. Any expected or unexpected adverse events. c. Humane endpoints established, signs
  monitored, and monitoring frequency (state explicitly if the study had no humane endpoints).
- **17 (Interpretation/scientific implications).** a. Interpretation of results relative to
  objectives, hypotheses, current theory, and other relevant literature. b. Study limitations,
  including potential sources of bias, model limitations, and imprecision.
- **18 (Generalisability/translation).** Whether, and how, the findings are likely to generalise
  to other species or experimental conditions, including relevance to human biology where
  appropriate.
- **19 (Protocol registration).** Whether a protocol (research question, key design features,
  analysis plan) was prepared before the study, and if/where registered.
- **20 (Data access).** Whether and where study data are available.
- **21 (Declaration of interests).** a. Potential conflicts of interest, financial and
  non-financial (state explicitly if none). b. All funding sources (including grant identifiers)
  and the funder's role in design, analysis, and reporting.

## Evidence to look for

An "Animals" or "Animal welfare" statement naming an ethics/IACUC/Home Office approval and licence
number; species/strain/sex/age details early in Methods; randomisation and blinding language;
housing/husbandry details; a stated primary outcome measure; exact n per group (not a range) in
Results; a humane-endpoints statement.
