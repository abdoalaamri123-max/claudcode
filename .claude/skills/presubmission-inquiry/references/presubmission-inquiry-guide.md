# Presubmission Inquiry Guide

Read this when drafting. Everything here is grounded in the journal's own website; never invent an editor, an email, or a policy.

## Part A - Find the pre-submission process

Not every journal offers pre-submission enquiries, and those that do differ in channel.

- Search for the journal's author guide and pre-submission information (journal name plus "presubmission enquiry" / "presubmission inquiry" / "guide to authors"), and fetch the official page.
- Determine: does the journal accept pre-submission enquiries? Through what channel (an option inside the online submission system, an email to the editorial office, a web form)? Any expected content (a paragraph, the abstract, a cover-letter-style note)?
- If the journal explicitly does not offer pre-submission enquiries, stop and tell the author to proceed to full submission; do not draft an enquiry that has nowhere to go.
- Record the source URL for what you found.

## Part B - Identify the handling editor

- Fetch the journal's editorial board / editors page (often "Editorial Board", "Editors", "Associate Editors", or subject-editor listings).
- Match the paper's topic to the editor whose stated expertise or subject section fits best. Note the specific reason for the match (the editor's listed area vs the paper's subfield).
- Fallbacks, in order, when there is no clear subject match: the section/deputy editor for the closest area, the editorial office / managing editor, then the editor-in-chief. State which fallback you used and why.
- Some journals route all enquiries centrally; in that case address the editorial office rather than guessing an individual.
- Confirm any email address from the site. Do not construct an address from a name and a domain; if no address is shown, flag it.
- Record for the report: editor name, role, match reason, contact channel, and the source page URL.

## Part C - Letter structure (keep it short)

A pre-submission enquiry is brief; editors triage many. Aim for a few short paragraphs.

1. **Salutation** to the identified editor ("Dear Dr. [Name],") or the office ("Dear Editor,").
2. **Opening:** one sentence stating this is a pre-submission enquiry about a manuscript the authors are considering submitting, with the working title and article type.
3. **The core:** two to four sentences distilling the key finding, why it is significant, and why it is of broad interest beyond the immediate subfield. Do not paste a full abstract unless the journal's process expects one; if it does, append the abstract below the letter.
4. **The ask:** explicitly ask whether the editor considers the work potentially suitable and would welcome a full submission.
5. **Close:** a brief thanks and the corresponding author's name and institutional contact.

Keep out the detailed methods, results, and the full declarations/reviewer lists; those belong in the first-submission cover letter, not an enquiry.

## Grounding and flags

Every factual element (editor, channel, policy) must trace to the site, with the source noted. Anything not confirmable is flagged rather than filled in. If the process or the right editor cannot be determined from the site, say so and ask the author how they would like to proceed.
